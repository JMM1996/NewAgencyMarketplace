# Strategic Brainstorm: Making NewAgencyMarketplace a Success

*Prepared: 29 January 2026*

---

## The Opportunity

You're entering a market with **massive unmet demand**:

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| UK care staffing market | £3.8 billion |
| Unfilled care home posts | 111,000+ |
| Homecare vacancy rate | 10%+ |
| Annual turnover | 335,000 leavers |
| Projected additional posts needed by 2040 | 200,000-470,000 |

The timing is good. The government has just ended overseas recruitment for care workers (May 2025), which will tighten supply and increase demand for efficient matching platforms.

- Claims 30% cost savings vs traditional agencies
- 94% shift fill rate (when posted 1 week ahead)

### Traditional Agencies
Reed, Hays, Newcross Healthcare, and dozens of regional agencies still dominate but are being disrupted by tech platforms.

## What's Working for Winners

Based on research, successful platforms share these traits:

### 1. Solve for Both Sides
- Care homes want: reliability, cost savings, fast fill rates
- Care workers want: flexibility, fair pay, respect, career growth

Florence wins because workers love the app (choose shifts, see pay upfront, get paid fast) AND care homes save 30% vs agencies.

### 2. Technology as Differentiator
- AI matching based on skills, experience, location
- Mobile-first (workers live on phones)
- Instant notifications for new shifts
- One-tap applications

### 3. Build an Ecosystem (Not Just a Marketplace)
Lottie became #1 by adding:
- CRM for care providers
- Employee benefits platform
- Data insights tools

This creates stickiness and multiple revenue streams.

### 4. Trust Through Verification
Workers with verified credentials get booked more. Care homes need confidence in who they're hiring.

## What Care Workers Want

| Priority | How to Win Them |
|----------|-----------------|
| Flexibility | Let them set availability, pick shifts, control their schedule |
| Fair pay | Transparent rates, no hidden deductions, quick payment |
| Respect | Good UX, clear communication, treat them as professionals |
| Career growth | Training links, certification tracking, work history |
| Security | Clear terms, fair treatment, dispute resolution |

**Warning**: 71% of gig workers value flexibility, but research shows they'd take a 14% pay cut for more job security. Don't assume everyone wants pure gig work.

## 5 Ideas to Differentiate

### 1. "Local First" Positioning
Florence is nationwide and can feel impersonal. Position as the platform that knows your region - start hyperlocal, build density, then expand.

**Why it works**: Care is local. A worker in Birmingham doesn't care about shifts in London. Density > breadth early on.

### 2. Reliability Score
Create a visible "Reliability Score" for workers based on:
- Shifts completed vs cancelled
- On-time arrivals
- Care home ratings

Care homes can filter by reliability. Workers are incentivised to maintain high scores. This directly addresses the #1 complaint.

### 3. "Favourites" from Day One
Let care homes save favourite workers and offer them shifts first. Simple feature, but creates loyalty on both sides. Florence has this - you should too.

### 4. Instant Pay (When You Monetise)
Offer same-day or next-day payment as a premium feature. Many care workers live paycheck to paycheck. This is a genuine differentiator.

### 5. Compliance Made Easy
The care sector is heavily regulated (DBS checks, right to work, qualifications). Build compliance tracking into the platform so care homes can prove due diligence. This is boring but valuable.

## Critical Success Factors

### Supply-Side First
You need workers before care homes will use you. A care home will try your platform once - if no one applies, they won't return.

**Action**: Recruit 50-100 care workers in your launch area before going live with care homes.

### Geographic Density
A platform with 1,000 workers spread across the UK is useless. A platform with 100 workers in one city is valuable.

**Action**: Pick ONE geographic area and dominate it before expanding.

### Speed to First Match
The first time a care home posts a shift and gets applicants within hours, they're hooked. The first time they post and hear crickets, they're gone.

**Action**: Seed early shifts, manually match if needed, whatever it takes to prove the concept works.

### Retention Over Acquisition
A care home that posts every week is worth 50x more than one that posts once. A worker who completes 10 shifts is worth 50x more than one who signs up and never books.

**Action**: Track and optimise for repeat usage, not just sign-ups.

## Risks to Watch

| Risk | Mitigation |
|------|------------|
| Florence is entrenched | Go local, be nimble, offer better service |
| Low margins at 15% | Keep costs low, automate everything possible |
| Worker exploitation concerns | Fair terms, transparent practices, build trust |
| Regulatory changes | Stay close to industry news, build compliance in |
| Two-sided marketplace cold start | Focus supply side first, then demand |

---

## Suggested Next Steps

1. **Validate demand locally** - Talk to 5 care homes in your target area. What do they currently pay agencies? How often do they need cover? What's broken?

2. **Understand workers** - Talk to 10 self-employed care workers. Where do they find shifts now? What would make them switch?

3. **Pick your launch geography** - One city or region. Where do you have connections?

4. **Define your "unfair advantage"** - What can you offer that Florence can't? Local knowledge? Better service? Specific niche?

5. **Set a launch metric** - "10 completed shift bookings in 90 days" or similar. Something concrete to aim for.

## Final Thought

Florence has 90,000 workers and £35m in funding. You won't out-feature them.

But you can:
- Move faster
- Care more about a specific geography
- Build relationships they can't
- Offer personal service at a scale they've abandoned

The care market is massive and growing. There's room for more than one player. The question is: what's YOUR wedge into this market?
